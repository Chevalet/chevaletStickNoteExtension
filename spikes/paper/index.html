Why the shadow root matters

This page sets * { box-sizing: border-box !important } and a serif font-family with !important on body. Neither reaches the notes: they live in a closed shadow root on a host with all: initial.

Every pixel that is not covered by a note hit-tests straight through to this text. Try selecting this paragraph while a note overlaps it — the selection works normally, because the host and its layers are pointer-events: none and only the note itself is auto.

What to look for

Grab a note by its header and move it slowly: it should follow almost exactly, with a barely perceptible lean. Now flick it. The sheet should lag, tilt into the direction of travel, shear slightly, and curl at the corner — then settle with one soft overshoot rather than snapping dead.

Grab a top corner and yank downward. The lever term should swing the note around the grab point, the way a real sheet of paper pivots when you drag it by one edge.

Watch the fps read-out. With nothing moving it should say idle · 0 animating: the shared requestAnimationFrame loop cancels itself when the last spring settles, so an idle tab full of notes costs nothing at all.

More filler

A photocopier degrades an image a little every generation. Contrast climbs, midtones collapse into pure black and white, dust on the platen becomes part of the artwork. Zine makers leaned into it: if the third-generation copy looked rougher, you made the fourth.

That is the target look here — high-contrast ink on saturated stock, halftone dots coarse enough to see, a hard unblurred drop shadow, and edges that were torn rather than cut.

The important constraint is that none of it may cost anything per frame. The tear is a path generated once from a seeded PRNG. The grain is one tile shared by every note, multiplied over the paper. The corner curl is five pre-baked paths cross-faded by opacity. Only transform and opacity ever change while a note is moving.
